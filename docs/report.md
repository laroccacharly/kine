# Montréal Robotics Seed-Funding Report

**Research date:** August 20, 2026  
**Funding window:** August 20, 2024–August 20, 2026  
**Geographic scope:** Montréal-based companies, including companies with a substantial Montréal office when they are headquartered jointly elsewhere  
**Funding scope:** Pre-seed, seed, and seed-extension financings

## Executive summary

This research identified **four confirmed companies** and **one probable company** matching the requested combination of Montréal presence, robotics or physical-AI relevance, and a recent seed-stage financing.

The clearest direct robotics matches are **Haply Robotics**, **Autonomique**, and **Uncharted Dynamics**. All three are positioned in the emerging physical-AI stack: Haply builds haptic control and data-capture hardware, Autonomique develops intelligence for industrial robots, and Uncharted Dynamics supplies physics simulation and training infrastructure for robot learning.

**Relocalize** is also a confirmed financing match, although it is better described as an autonomous-manufacturing or cleantech company that makes extensive use of robotics than as a conventional robotics vendor. **Wisemotion Robotics** is a strong probable match: its Montréal location, robotics focus, and $800,000 pre-seed financing are supported by public materials, but no precise announcement date or investor list was found.

## Selection criteria

A company was included when the available evidence supported all of the following:

1. It is based in Montréal or maintains a material Montréal operation.
2. Its core product concerns robots, robot intelligence, robot simulation, teleoperation, or substantially autonomous physical systems.
3. It announced or disclosed a pre-seed, seed, seed-plus, or seed-extension financing between August 20, 2024 and August 20, 2026.

The category was interpreted broadly enough to include the physical-AI software stack, not only companies that manufacture complete robots. Borderline companies are explicitly labelled rather than silently treated as equivalent to pure-play robotics vendors.

## Company profiles

### 1. Haply Robotics

**Why it qualifies:** Haply is a Montréal-based haptics and robotics company. Its hardware and software capture pressure, resistance, and interaction forces, enabling people to manipulate digital environments and physical robots with precise force feedback. Its products support teleoperation, robot learning, medical simulation, 3D design, and other physical-AI workflows.

**Recent financing:** In early February 2026, Haply closed an **$11.75 million USD ($16 million CAD) seed-plus equity round**. The financing brought its total capital raised to more than $20 million CAD.

**Investors:**

- Sound Media Ventures, lead investor
- Amazon Industrial Innovation Fund
- Hanwha Asset Management Venture Fund
- Two Small Fish Ventures
- BDC Capital Deep Tech Venture Fund, follow-on investor

**Use of proceeds:** Expansion, manufacturing capacity, hiring, and a planned California office. At the time of the announcement, Haply reportedly employed roughly 50 people, manufactured its devices at its Plateau-area Montréal headquarters, and served more than 150 customers.

**Assessment:** This is the strongest match in the dataset. The company is unquestionably Montréal-based, its product is directly relevant to robotics and teleoperation, and the round amount, stage, date, and investor group were all reported clearly by an independent technology publication.

**Primary research source:** [BetaKit — Haply Robotics raises $16 million to build the “steering wheels” for physical AI](https://betakit.com/haply-robotics-raises-16-million-to-build-the-steering-wheels-for-physical-ai/)

### 2. Uncharted Dynamics

**Why it qualifies:** Uncharted Dynamics is a Montréal-based embodied-AI infrastructure company developing a high-fidelity multi-body dynamics solver and physics-grounded datasets for robot learning. Its technology focuses on difficult contact-rich problems such as deformation, friction, soft-rigid coupling, manipulation, and assembly.

Rather than building robot bodies, the company aims to provide the physically accurate simulation and data layer used to train robot intelligence and future world models.

**Recent financing:** On May 13, 2026, the company announced a **multimillion-dollar seed round**. The exact amount was not disclosed.

**Investors:**

- K2VC, lead investor

No other participating investors were identified in the public announcement.

**Use of proceeds:** Continued development of the company's multi-body dynamics solver and expansion in the North American robotics market.

**Assessment:** This is a strong robotics-infrastructure match. The announcement explicitly states the round, stage, date, Montréal base, lead investor, and intended robotics-market use. Confidence is slightly lower than for Haply because the detailed public evidence located during the search originated with the company and was distributed through a press-release service; an independent report confirming the round was not found.

**Primary research source:** [Access Newswire — Uncharted Dynamics closes seed round led by K2VC](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/computers-technology-and-internet/uncharted-dynamics-closes-seed-round-led-by-k2vc-to-build-physics-1166177)

### 3. Autonomique

**Why it qualifies:** Autonomique develops physical-AI software that controls industrial robots. Its platform is designed to perform complete manufacturing workflows involving perception, planning, manipulation, and adaptation, while integrating with third-party robot hardware.

The company operates from both Montréal and Menlo Park, California, with its team reportedly split roughly evenly between the two offices. In 2026, its software progressed from pilot deployment to production use assembling automotive chassis and suspension components at an F&P Manufacturing facility in Ontario.

**Recent financing:** By June 17, 2026, Autonomique had closed the **first tranche of an undisclosed seed round**.

**Investors:**

- White Star Capital
- Innovobot
- Garage Capital
- Inovia Capital
- Founders of Clearpath Robotics and Otto Motors

Individual cheque sizes and the total round target were not disclosed.

**Use of proceeds:** Not explicitly itemized in the source, although the company was commercializing and deploying its physical-AI system in industrial production.

**Assessment:** This is another direct robotics match, with unusually strong commercial evidence for a seed-stage company: the technology was already operating on a customer production line. The main caveat is that the company is dual-location rather than solely headquartered in Montréal, and the disclosed financing represents only the first tranche of a seed round.

**Primary research source:** [BetaKit — Autonomique says backflipping robots are not the point](https://betakit.com/autonomique-says-backflipping-robots-are-not-the-point/)

### 4. Relocalize

**Why it qualifies:** Relocalize is a Montréal-based autonomous-manufacturing startup developing compact, distributed “dark” microfactories. Its facilities use AI, industrial automation, and remotely controlled robots to manufacture packaged food and cold-chain products with minimal on-site human intervention.

The company is commercially framed primarily as cleantech and manufacturing infrastructure. It is included because robotics are central to the operation of its product, but it should be treated as **robotics-adjacent**, not as a pure robotics platform company.

**Recent financing:** Relocalize closed a **$7 million CAD seed-extension round in March 2026**, following earlier seed closings in 2023 and 2024. The company separately secured $2.5 million in equipment financing from CWV Sustainable Royalties; that equipment financing is not part of the $7 million equity round.

**Investors:**

- Desjardins Capital, lead investor
- Waterpoint Lane
- RGS Ice

**Use of proceeds:** Commercialization and deployment of autonomous microfactories, including a Montréal facility near the Lachine Canal and another facility in Florida. The planned Montréal plant was described as a near-total-darkness operation without in-person production shifts.

**Assessment:** The financing facts and Montréal presence are well supported. Whether it belongs on a robotics-company list depends on the intended definition: it is an operator and developer of robotic autonomous factories, rather than a general-purpose robot or robotics-software supplier.

**Primary research source:** [BetaKit — Relocalize to build fully autonomous “dark” microfactory in Montréal](https://betakit.com/relocalize-to-build-fully-autonomous-dark-microfactory-in-montreal/)

### 5. Wisemotion Robotics

**Why it probably qualifies:** Wisemotion develops AI- and physics-informed motion-planning software for industrial manipulators, palletizing robots, mobile platforms, and humanoids. The company appears in TandemLaunch's portfolio as a Montréal robotics startup founded in 2025. Public recruiting materials describe its core product as a hardware-agnostic motion planner intended to reduce robot-programming and deployment time.

The project appears to have previously operated under the name **movwize** during incubation. A public post dated April 17, 2025 announced TandemLaunch's partnership with movwize and described the same Purdue University-derived, physics-informed robot-navigation technology. Later public materials use the Wisemotion name.

**Recent financing:** Public recruiting and founder materials state that the company secured **$800,000 in pre-seed funding**. The currency was not specified in the materials found, and no formal dated closing announcement was located.

**Investors:** Not publicly disclosed. TandemLaunch incubated the company and lists Wisemotion in its portfolio, but the available evidence does not establish the complete investor group or allow TandemLaunch's exact investment to be stated confidently.

**Assessment:** The company, Montréal location, robotics focus, and financing amount are well supported across several public materials. It remains a probable rather than fully confirmed result because the financing date, currency, closing structure, and investor list were not formally disclosed. The 2025 incubation timeline makes it highly likely that the round falls inside the research window.

**Sources:**

- [TandemLaunch portfolio — Wisemotion](https://www.tandemlaunch.com/portfolio)
- [Wisemotion Robotics website](https://wisemotion.ai/)

## Market observations

### Recent activity is concentrated in physical AI

The strongest matches cluster around the interface between artificial intelligence and machines acting in the physical world:

- Haply captures human touch and motion for teleoperation and learning.
- Autonomique supplies perception, planning, and manipulation intelligence for deployed robots.
- Uncharted Dynamics produces physics-grounded simulation and training infrastructure.
- Wisemotion focuses on efficient robot-motion planning.

Together, these companies represent complementary layers of a robotics stack rather than four competing robot manufacturers.

### Round structures are becoming less standardized

Only Uncharted Dynamics used the simple label “seed round.” Haply called its financing “seed-plus,” Autonomique disclosed only a first tranche, and Relocalize continued extending a seed round begun in earlier years. This makes stage-based searches less reliable unless individual financing structures are read carefully.

### Public disclosure is uneven

Haply's round was highly transparent, including its amount and investor syndicate. Uncharted Dynamics disclosed its lead but not its amount. Autonomique disclosed investors but not the amount. Wisemotion disclosed an amount through recruiting and founder materials without a formal financing announcement. Any investor-prospecting workflow should preserve these confidence differences.

### Grants are an important but distinct source of robotics capital

Several Québec robotics companies—including ARA Robotics, Sami Robotics, and Tessellate Robotics—have received meaningful public grants, awards, or repayable government contributions. These are useful ecosystem signals but should not be combined with venture seed rounds when comparing investor activity or dilution.

## Research method and limitations

The research used Exa to review **225 search-result slots across four search workstreams**, followed by full-page retrieval of the most relevant sources. The workstreams covered:

1. English-language Montréal robotics and seed-funding announcements.
2. French-language Québec and Montréal financing coverage.
3. Venture-fund, accelerator, and portfolio-company discovery.
4. Candidate-specific verification and searches for missing or contradictory evidence.

Results were deduplicated by company and tested against geography, sector, funding stage, and date. Independent reporting was preferred where available, followed by company announcements, investor portfolio pages, and public professional materials.

This is a high-confidence shortlist, not a guarantee of completeness. Private seed rounds may never be announced, and very young companies sometimes disclose funding only in recruiting posts or founder profiles. Montréal's ecosystem also has fuzzy geographic boundaries: companies in Laval, Longueuil, Brossard, Varennes, and the wider metropolitan region were not treated as Montréal proper unless a material Montréal operation was documented.

## Conclusion

For direct outreach or deeper diligence, the recommended priority order is:

1. **Haply Robotics** — clearest and most fully documented direct robotics financing.
2. **Autonomique** — direct physical-AI company with production deployment and a strong seed syndicate.
3. **Uncharted Dynamics** — newly funded embodied-AI infrastructure company, although its financing is currently supported mainly by its own announcement.
4. **Wisemotion Robotics** — very early and potentially interesting, but financing details require direct verification.
5. **Relocalize** — compelling autonomous-manufacturing prospect when the mandate includes robotics-enabled companies.
